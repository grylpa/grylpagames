# Wolves — Game Design

## Concept

A herding game. The player is a sheepdog patrolling a fenced farm. Sheep start inside a central compound; the compound fence degrades over time and sheep may wander out. Walking near a scared sheep triggers A*-pathfinding back to the compound. Wolves (hostile agents) can also appear. The player must keep the sheep alive until the level ends.

## Game Flow

1. Level starts: board is built with one compound room filled with sheep
2. The compound fence starts intact; pieces fall off on a timer
3. The outer farm fence also degrades separately on a slower timer
4. Sheep wander freely inside their room; if the player comes within `dist_to_scare` tiles they path back to the compound center
5. A sheep is "lost" if it wanders off the board or off the playable field (not a room and not a field tile)
6. Each lost sheep reduces `game.lives_left` by 1
7. If all sheep are lost → level fails
8. The level runs for its `level_time`; reaching the end of the clock ends it
9. It is PASSED on the share of the flock still inside at that moment (see "Passing a level")

## Controls

- Arrow keys / touch: move the player (sheepdog); Space: stop; Tab: next dog (several dogs)
- **Several dogs** (a level's `dogs`, 1-3, default 1): a SWIPE goes to the dog nearest where it starts
  (`_on_path_drawn`); with several dogs a swipe starting more than `SWIPE_REACH_MANY` (4) cells from all
  of them is ignored -- one dog keeps its old reach of 9. The keyboard drives the ACTIVE dog: the one
  last swiped, or picked with TAB, which cycles through the dogs. SPACE stops the active dog, on every
  level (Space switching dogs was tried briefly and lost the stop).
  The active dog wears a gold CONTOUR hugging its body and wagging tail, with a soft glow outside it,
  breathing (`beast_art.gd` `_active_contour`, asking `player.gd` `mark_strength()`), fading in and out
  over a quarter second as the choice moves; with one dog there is none. (A ring on the ground was tried
  first.)
- F / L: faster / slower
- N: new game
- M: main menu
- H: help

## Board Layout

- `board_margin = 3` tiles of non-playable border on each side
- One compound room centered on the board (room 0)
- Field tiles fill the inside of the farm border (all non-room tiles within the margin)
- Farm border = outermost ring of field tiles; carries fence flags on its edges
- The board is 25 x 25 on desktop and **19 x 29 on a phone** (`_apply_level`): a phone's screen is tall,
  and the square board fitted to its width made every cell ~32 units with a third of the screen forest
- The first dog starts at the top center of the field (above the compound), a second at the bottom
  center, a third at the left (`add_player`, each on the nearest free field cell)

## Fence Degradation

Two independent timers (real-time `game.game_time` ms):

| Fence | Config key | What breaks |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| Compound | `compund_fence_interval` | Random fence piece on compound wall → sheep can wander out |
| Farm | `farm_fence_interval` | Random piece of outer farm border → wolves/enemies can enter |

Fence pieces are directional flags (`pipe.fences[0..3]`) on individual tiles. `show_hide_walls()` redraws wall visuals after each removal.

**On a new board every clock starts again** (`create_board`): the fence timers AND the wolves'
(`time_to_next_wolf`, `time_to_play_wolf`). `game.game_time` restarts at 0 on a new board, and the
wolves' were left from the last one -- after a lost level ("Oh no!") the next wolf was due ~37 s into the
replay. The fence layer (`_fence_node`, made once) sits at `z_index` 1, above the tiles: a rebuilt board's
tiles come after it in the tree and were drawn over the fences.

## Sheep Behavior

- Sheep are agents of `agent_type = 0`
- Speed: `agent_max_speed_scale × sheep_speed_cfg × rng(0.8, 2.0)`
- Default AI: keep current direction; turn at walls or when blocked
- **Scared state**: when player comes within `dist_to_scare` tiles while sheep is outside its room → A* path back to `rooms[0].get_center()`. Path cost: straight = 1, turn = 20, wall/brick/agent = blocked.
- `check_if_sheep_is_lost(agent)`: sheep is lost if it wanders off-board or off the playable area (`!is_field && room_id < 0`)
- `game.lives_left = number_of_sheep_alive`; HUD shows remaining sheep

## Sheep animation

Sheep use the `Sheep` animation in the shared `scenes/head_anim.tscn`. It is a 24-frame sway cut
from `art/sheep_sway.png`, generated by rotating the upright `sheep2-4x.png` about the image
center point (64, 64) — the pivot the original art was drawn around — through
`angle(i) = -10.02 * cos(2*PI*i/24)`.

That reproduces exactly what the original four frames `[sheep1, sheep2, sheep3, sheep2]` did
(-10 deg, 0, +10 deg, 0) and keeps the 0.8 s cycle (`speed = 30` over 24 frames instead of
`speed = 5` over 4), but the largest step is 2.7 deg per frame instead of 10, so the sway reads as
motion rather than three discrete poses.

## All the animals are drawn, in one style

`scripts/beast_art.gd`, a `Node2D` added as a child of the animal's `Head` (the dog in `player.gd`
`_ready`; a sheep or a wolf in `agent.gd` `_dress_as`). The head's own sprite -- the sheep sprite, or
the app's generic blob tinted blue for the dog and yellow for the wolves -- is made invisible with
`self_modulate` rather than removed, so the head's rotation still steers the animal and the bark /
eat / scare scale tweens still play on it.

ONE style for all three, seen from above, facing +x, in level pixels (the head's scale is undone):
a soft shadow on the grass, one dark outline of the same weight (`OUT_W`) round every shape, flat
fills lit from the top left of the SCREEN. The dog and the wolf are each ONE SILHOUETTE from tail root
to nose (`_silhouette`: body, neck and head in a single outline), so each reads as one animal and not
as a body with a head stuck on, and they are drawn at `BEAST_SIZE` (0.86) of a sheep's scale, so nose to
tail fits ONE CELL (at 1.22, with long tails, they were 1.6 cells long), and `BEAST_GIRTH` (1.7) wider
across than along, so on a phone they are not thin strokes; their tails are short for the same reason. Tails are curves, not sticks (`_wavy_tail`: each segment turns a little further than the
last, and the wag is a WAVE travelling from root to tip, so the tail bends as it swings), with filled
fur tufts along their edges (`_tail_tufts`).

- **sheep**: a THICK cream fleece, two rings of round tufts and more inside, each lit on its top left
  with a little curl in it, a scalloped outline; a dark face with two little ears sticking out
  sideways.
- **dog**: a border collie -- a long, even body with a slight waist, a domed skull and a tapering
  muzzle; black with a sheen on the lit side, a white collar and a white blaze to the muzzle, amber
  eyes, soft ears folded forward over the skull, a long feathered tail curving to one side with a
  white tip, wagging fast.
- **wolf**: lean flanks and heavy shoulders, a broad skull and a long NARROW muzzle with a dark nose;
  gray with a lighter lit side, a dark saddle running up the neck into a mask between amber slanted
  eyes, pale cheeks and muzzle front, tall pointed ears standing clear of the head, shaggy ruff tufts
  round the shoulders, a long bushy tail held low with a dark tip, swaying slowly.

History: the sheep were a sprite (an egg with fleece dashes); the dog and wolves were a detailed
shaded version with striding paws (no feet now: from above they only cluttered), then flat eggs in
the sheep's style ("the wolves look like mice"), then a body-plus-head version stretched sideways,
whose tail -- a straight stroke with a white bulb, swinging round its root -- read as "a reverse
pendulum", and whose wolf "doesn't look like a wolf". Check new art large (a scratch scene with the
three animals at 5x) before judging it at game size: at game size details disappear.

**The animals are PICTURES** (2026-11-13). Drawn as shapes every frame, a flock of 65 sheep (~120
antialiased circles each) took ~42 ms of render time a frame on a laptop GPU -- 5 fps; on a phone the dog
(timed by the clock) jumped, and it was smooth only once few sheep were left. So each kind is RENDERED
ONCE into an atlas (`_bake_atlas`: a SubViewport, `SHEEP_ANGLES` 16 headings x 6 fleeces for the sheep, 1
for the dog and the wolf, at `SHEEP_RES` 2x, the dog's and wolf's already stretched by their girth), and
each animal draws the picture at the heading nearest its own (`_draw_picture`, which undoes the picture's
heading and girth), so the light still falls from the top left as it turns. The dog's and wolf's
pictures are their BODIES; the shadow (one cached polygon) and the wagging tail are drawn live. A sheep
redraws only when it has turned. Until an atlas is ready (a frame or two) the animals draw as shapes.
Render time with a full flock: ~3 ms; 60 fps.

**Turns are quick and come as a step starts.** `set_rots` does not set the head's rotation: `agent.gd`
`_ease_turn` and `player.gd` `_process` swing it round to `angles[0]` (`TURN_RATE` 45/s: a quarter turn
in well under 0.1 s), and `angles[0]` is set from the step's direction as each step STARTS -- never from
the frame-to-frame drift. At 12/s the animals visibly turned while walking. A freshly placed animal
(`set_pos`) faces its way at once. A wolf about to eat turns to face the sheep where it IS
(`mark_eating(agent.position)`), and bites only after 0.15 s, once facing it.
**The shared head script stays ON** (`scripts/head_anim.gd`): it gives every head its smooth turns and
rounded corners, and it must not be switched off -- doing so (2026-11-13, to let a wolf face its prey) made
every turn abrupt. It faces a head along its recent path, so it RESTS only while a wolf eats
(`mark_eating` -> `$Head.set_process(false)`, back on when the bite is done); meanwhile `_ease_turn`
turns the head toward the sheep at the script's own steady pace (a quarter turn in 0.1 s).

## Movement (2026-11-13)

Everything moves CELL TO CELL at a constant speed, and a run of cells is one even glide:
- **The level's moves run every frame** (`main.gd` `_process` -> `level.tick()`; the 50 ms `GameTick` now
  only keeps the game clock). On the timer, an animal that reached its cell waited up to 50 ms for the
  next one, then lurched on.
- **Sheep and wolves** (`agent.gd` `_move`): one cell per step period at
  `tile * speed_scale * _speed_mul / major_tick_time_ms / time_scale`, by the frame's own time. On the
  cell (`can_set_target_pos`: within 0.5 units), the animal asks for its next cell AT ONCE
  (`level.step_agent_now` -> `_step_agent`, the old body of `tick`'s loop) and carries the rest of the
  frame's step into it; given it a frame later it stopped short at every cell. An animal that finds
  nowhere to go retries after 50 ms (`idle_cell` / `idle_until_ms`), not every frame (the path searches).
- **The dog** (`player.gd`): a step that follows straight on from the last starts where and WHEN that one
  ended (`set_target_pos`), ends exactly ON its cell, and on arriving the dog asks for its next step in the
  same frame (`level.step_player_now(self)`).

## A* Pathfinding

- Used for scared sheep routing back to compound
- `calc_cost_to_move_to()` respects fence flags, bricks, and agent occupancy
- Straight-line movement costs 1; direction changes cost 20 (encourages minimal turning)
- If path fails: fall back to greedy step toward player direction

## Scoring

- Collecting a coin: `+coin_value` (1 or 2 depending on border vs interior coin position)
- Lost sheep: `−1`
- Level complete (win): `+min(5, 60 − elapsed_seconds)` score, `+min(10, 60 − elapsed_seconds)` time bonus
- Correct answer: `+1` score, `+5` time
- Wrong answer: `−1` score, `−5` time

## Lives

- `game.lives_left` = current sheep count (set in `add_sheep()`, decremented on each loss)
- Live icon = sheep sprite (`res://art/sheep2-4x.png`)
- No explicit "game over on lives = 0" — `check_if_no_sheep_left()` calls `level_is_done(false)` when all sheep are gone

## Level Config (`WolvesLevelConfig`)

Defined in `level_config.gd` as `class_name WolvesLevelConfig`.

| Level | Room | Compound fence (ms) | Farm fence (ms) | Sheep speed | Pct sheep | Wolves interval | Dist to scare |
|-------|------|---------------------|-----------------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | 9×9 | 4000 | 8000 | 0.3× | 30% | 20 | 3 |
| 2 | 11×11 | 2000 | 8000 | 0.3× | 30% | 10000 | 3 |
| 3 | 11×11 | 2000 | 7000 | 0.4× | 40% | 4000 | 3 |
| 4 | 11×11 | 1500 | 5000 | 0.5× | 50% | 3000 | 2 |
| 5 | 11×11 | 1000 | 1000 | 0.5× | 80% | 1000 | 2 |

`dogs` (1-3, default 1) sets how many dogs a level has; every level has two.

`pct_sheep` x room area = number of sheep placed. Color per level is set in `_cfg["color"]`.
`pass_pct` is the share of the flock that must survive: 50, 60, 65, 70, 75 for levels 1-5.

## Passing a level

Lasting the level is not the same as passing it. When the clock runs out, `level_is_done(true)`
measures the flock:

```
pct = 100 * get_num_sheep_left() / _sheep_at_level_start
passed = pct >= WolvesLevelConfig.pass_pct_for(level)
```

Below the bar the SAME level is played again; at or above it, the next one. Before this,
`need_to_increase_level` was set unconditionally on the win path — surviving the clock with one
sheep left promoted the player exactly as fast as keeping the whole flock, and the "Sheep saved"
line on the card decided nothing.

**`_sheep_at_level_start` is measured, not computed.** `pct_sheep` fills each room by AREA, and how
many sheep that comes to depends on the maze that was generated, so the denominator can only be
read off the board. It is stamped at the end of `create_board()`, right after `add_sheep()` — NOT
in `new_game()`, because `create_board()` awaits and `new_game()` does not await it, so a stamp
there reads an empty flock. (It did: the probe measured 0 sheep.)

Losing the whole flock (`check_if_no_sheep_left`) is the same failure arriving early: it clears
`need_to_increase_level` and rolls the score back, then shows the "Oh no!" panel it always did.

The last level (5) is judged the same way but never promotes.

## "complete!" only when it was

`show_level_done_popup` is called with the gate result as its `passed` argument, so the card reads
"Level N complete!" with a check badge or **"Level N not passed"** with none. The body is
`Sheep saved: 7 of 9`, `Flock kept: 77%`, `Total score: N`, and then what happens next:

- passed -> `Level passed — on to level N.`
- failed -> `You need to keep at least 60% of the flock to pass to the next level.`

`MainGlobals.global_level_is_done()` takes the same result, so the fanfare does not play over a
level that was not passed.

## A failed level earns nothing

`_score_at_level_start` is stamped at the top of `new_game()` — after the rollback, so consecutive
failures all measure from the same point — and a level that misses the gate goes back to it
(`_rollback_score_on_next_level`). Otherwise the gate is a scoring exploit: the score is cumulative
across a session, so every failed attempt banked its +50 level bonus and the retry cost nothing.

The rollback lands on Continue rather than when the level ends, because watching the score drop out
from under a summary you are still reading is alarming. wolves does not emit
`game.sig_level_is_done`, so unlike the games that save a score row per level there is nothing to
swap the kept value in for.

## Difficulty Scaling

`times_per_difficulty = 3` (3 stages per level). Stage progression tracked as `difficulty` integer.

- Board size: fixed 25×25 (19×29 on a phone)
- `num_rooms = min(1, ...)` — currently only 1 room (compound)
- `agent_max_speed_scale = min(0.4 + 0.2 × (level − 1), 2.0)`
- On level-up (new level, not just new stage): `game.add_life()`

## Camera

Two camera modes switchable via `zoom_camera(zoom_in)`:
- **Zoom out** (`zoom_in = false`): game-level camera, fits entire board. Always used in Wolves. On a
  phone it fits the FARM and a cell and a half all round it, where the wolves walk (the ring just outside
  the fence); fitted like the desktop board it left half a cell above and below.
- **Zoom in** (`zoom_in = true`): player-following camera, scaled to `room_max_size + 2` tiles wide. Available but not activated.

## Key Implementation Notes

- `_load_cfg()` maps current level to `WolvesLevelConfig.LEVELS[level - 1]`
- `board` is a 2D Array of `OneCell` objects; `OneCell.fences` delegates to `pipe.fences`
- Sheep use `board[p].has_agent = true` to block movement; player also sets `has_agent` on its cell
- `is_wall_between(p, q)` checks fence flags in both directions before allowing movement
- `can_player_go_to(q)` checks `is_field && room_id < 0 && !has_agent && !is_wall_between`
- `can_go_to(agent, q)` for sheep (type 0): checks `!has_agent && !is_wall_between` within the board
- **The dogs**: `players` is the list; `player` is the dog being handled at the moment (every per-dog step
  -- `move_player_on_tick` -> `_move_dog`, `step_player_now`, the swipe -- sets it and loops) and between
  those the ACTIVE dog (`active_dog`, `set_active_dog`, `_active_dog()`). `dist_to_player` is to the NEAREST dog, `_is_dog(a)` tests any of them, a keyboard
  turn waits on the dog itself (`player.next_dir`), and a dog never steps into another (`can_player_go_to`).
  `on_player_is_really_moving` counts running dogs for the feet sound.
- `game.initial_score = 100` — player starts with 100 points
- `BE.upsert_game_state` and `BE.send_event` — backend telemetry calls on new game and level done
- `game.progress_time_pos = 7` — time shown at HUD position 7

## Tutorial

Coached tutorial in `wolves/scripts/tutorial.gd`; see `docs/tutorials.md` for the framework.

The tutorial plays level 1, with both dogs: a "Two dogs" step spots the second one
and says a route goes to the dog nearest where you start drawing it. Its words follow the device
(`MainGlobals.is_mobile()`): on desktop, "your mouse", the arrow keys, Space to stop and Tab to pass the
arrows to the other dog; on a phone, "your finger" and no keys at all. The instructions screen does the
same (`main.gd` `_controls_text`).

- **Entry**: as for the other games; `WolvesG.starting_level` is saved/restored by hand.
- **Hooks in `level.gd`** (no-ops outside tutorial mode): `_on_path_drawn` emits `path_drawn`;
  scaring emits `scared_one` plus `scared_sheep` / `scared_wolf`; a wolf reaching a sheep emits
  `sheep_eaten`.
- **`tutorial_demo_route()`** builds the demo path from the game's own pathfinder (`game.astar`
  with `calc_cost_to_move_player_to`), then rounds the grid corners off. The first version was a
  hand-written zig-zag of fixed offsets: it took no account of the board, so it pointed straight
  through the farm fence, and three equal straight segments looked nothing like a finger drag.
- **`demo_path`**: the tutorial animates a hand tracing that route and then the dog following the
  same line, so the gesture is shown rather than described. "Trace a route" is an instruction players have had to act on
  nowhere else in the app, and words alone were not getting it across.
- **Keyboard is not step-by-step**: an arrow sets the dog walking that way until something stops
  it. The tutorial said "a step at a time", which was wrong.
- **Drawn-path movement**: wolves and storm are the only two games where
  `MainGlobals.draw_path_mode` is on. Everywhere else in the app a finger drag is a flick, a swipe
  answer, or a drag of an object — so this has to be taught explicitly, and the player draws one.
- **`_tutorial_setup()`** moves a single sheep out onto the open field via `_tutorial_field_spot()`.
  The tutorial asks the player to startle a stray back in, and at level start every sheep is still
  safely inside with the fence only just beginning to fail — so without this the player waits in
  front of an intact flock for a stray that may not come.

## The lawn

The ground is ONE continuous field of drawn grass over the whole board — `scripts/grass_field.gd`,
shared by the eleven grass games — not a tile. `level.gd`'s `_fit_ground_to_board()` is the whole
installation:

```gdscript
GrassField.fit(self, get_node_or_null("TextureRect") as CanvasItem, game, 21)
```

It hides the tiled `TextureRect` it replaces, attaches a `GrassField` control to the Level layer itself, as its first child so it
draws behind everything, sizes it to the board plus a four-tile margin (merged with the full canvas,
so a board smaller than the screen still has grass to the edges), and sows it. The seed is this
game's own — 21 — so no two games show the same field.

It is called twice: at the end of `_ready()`, so the lawn is already there before the first board is
built, and at the START of `create_board()`, for a level that changes the board's size. `fit()`
re-sows only when the rect actually changed, because the field is a `MultiMeshInstance2D` of tens to
hundreds of thousands of blades and building it is not something to redo between rounds.

Every empty cell used to carry its own 40x40 `grass.png`; `empty_space.gd`'s `_ready()` now hides it.
That per-cell sprite was the real reason the board looked tiled — the background alone was never
it — and the per-cell random rotation some of these games applied made it worse, because the tile
wraps seamlessly and turning a cell breaks the wrap.

`probe_lawn.gd` checks all eleven: the field exists, is the first child of its layer, is sown before
any board is built, covers the board and the canvas, retires the tiled ground only once it has
something in it, and that no cell shows its own grass again.

Inside the fence the ground is a flat translucent green wash (`pipe.gd`'s `FIELD_WASH`, drawn by the
pipe itself so it sits under the bricks, coins and fences). Field cells used to show `%PipeGrass` —
a randomly rotated, green-tinted copy of the same `grass.png` — which was a mosaic twice over. All
that tint ever said was "this side of the fence is the farm", so that is all that is left.

## No snake body

This game's agents are a head and nothing else. `player.gd` used to carry the whole
trailing-body rig copied from the delivery games — `body_ids`, `bodies`, `nbody_parts`, a
`time_back_positions` trail, `find_closest_dist()`, `add_body`/`remove_body`/`final_remove_body`, a
`Skeleton` `Line2D` to string the segments on, and a `tube_animation.tscn` built from
`agent_body2.png` / `agent_body3.png` — but the level always assigned an EMPTY `body_ids`, so the
build loop never ran and not one segment was ever created. Measured by running the game and counting
the nodes, not by reading it.

All of it is gone: the script, the `Skeleton` node in `player.tscn` (and the orphaned one in
`agent.tscn`), the
`tube_animation` scene and script, and the two PNGs. `angles` keeps a single entry, the head's
heading, which is all `set_rots()` ever read.

**parkem is the one game that really does grow a body** (four segments on level 1, two on level 2),
so its rig stays. Do not copy this game's `agent.gd` there, or the reverse.

## What this game measures

Session records are the v6 named-dictionary format (see `scripts/generic_game_util.gd`
and `scripts/session_stats.gd`). Metrics reset centrally in `reset(from_scratch)`.

Response times are handed to the shared session record as a whole distribution, not just a mean: `game.record_times()` in `main.gd::get_game_score()` stores spread, median, within-session slope and lapse count beside the mean. The spread is the point — it moves before the mean does.

**It used to record nothing.** `get_game_score()` returned `[didwin, aborted, level]` — not one
measurement — and `record_times($Level.times_to_answer)` was handed an array that `level.gd`
declares and never appends to, so the session stored `rt_n: 0` and stopped there. This was the
only game in the app whose Summary tab had no rows at all, and its category could never hear
from it.

It now records the **share of the flock still inside**, which is the level's own pass mark
(`_pct_flock_kept`, exposed as `pct_flock_kept_now()`). As a NAMED metric rather than the
positional column: `pct_correct` sits at index 4 and `rt_mean` at 3, so filling it positionally
would mean writing a response time this game does not have, and a Speed row reading 0 ms is
worse than no Speed row at all.

**Still missing, and it is the measurement this game most wants:** the interval from a sheep
getting out to the player reaching it. That is what a vigilance game is about — long quiet
stretches, then how fast you respond — and `times_to_answer` exists to hold it. The
`record_times` call is left in place for when it is filled.

and wolf's short tails and girth (`B_BEAST_GIRTH`). Redrawn 2026-11-13 with the farm; it showed the old
yellow-and-black taped fence on a plain pen wash.

History (2026-10-30): the tiles before this were all SIDE-ON. Four side-on wolves were drawn and
dropped (an assembly of discs, a slab-sided polygon, a bench-like barrel, honest proportions that
still did not look good); the dog went too; and the sheep-only tile that was left named the game by
what you protect, and read as a farm game. The game's own top-down animals answered it: they were
already drawn and approved, and they make the threat visible. The side-on `wolf()`, `sheep()` and
`dog()` are kept in `make_thumbs.py`.

## Sounds

Made for this game by `devtools/make_action_sounds.py` (synthesized, seeded; names in code kept): `start` a shepherd's two-note whistle (was a click), `feet` the sheepdog trotting (`wolves_steps.ogg`, a 1.2 s loop: soft paw pats landing in pairs, as a trot does, a brush of grass, and a collar tag jingling on every other beat, which makes it read as a dog at once; it replaced a Kenney grass footstep on 2026-11-01 -- a person's steps on grass were tried first and turned down). The sheep, dog and wolf recordings stay. The `ambient` is MUSIC since 2026-11-09 (`art/sounds/wolves_ambient.ogg`, `make_ambient_sounds.py` `wolves`: a hillside jig in 6/8 -- plucked guitar, bass, a tin-whistle tune, the flock's bells clonking here and there; -6 dB); it was a recording of sheep bells, heard as noise; a `swoosh` that was registered and never played is gone (2026-11-01). The bells (`ambient`) and the steps stop at the main menu too (`main.gd` `show_main_menu`): leaving a level part way used to leave the bells ringing over the menu.

**The field is a mown pasture** (`pasture.gdshader` on each field tile's sprite, set in `pipe.gd`'s `_ready`): a lighter, yellower green than the lawn outside the fence, soft broad patches, blade grain and darker tufts (it had mowing stripes, which read as horizontal bands), in world coordinates. It replaced a gray stone tile that read as asphalt. Mid-light on purpose: the white sheep and the dark wolves must both stand out. The compound keeps its per-level color, and `FIELD_WASH` still applies over it.

## Look

The animals are `beast_art.gd`'s (see "All the animals are drawn, in one style"); `MonsterArt` leaves a
head another drawing already hid. The farm round them (2026-11-12; it was a flat purple pen, thin dotted
yellow fences, brick blocks and a plain dark lawn):
- **Fences** (`level._draw_fences`, redrawn from `show_hide_walls()`): wooden POST-AND-RAIL, drawn from the
  cells' `fences` flags -- a post at each end of every fenced edge, two rails between, a soft shadow. The
  PEN's fence is painted white (natural wood all but vanished on its brown earth). A piece that BREAKS
  (`_note_broken`, called by `remove_one_compund_fence` / `remove_one_farm_fence` before the flag is
  cleared; `_broken` is cleared in `create_board`) is FALLEN FENCE: nothing between its posts, not even a
  stub, and two broken pieces lying loose in the grass, off the line and CLEAR OF BOTH POSTS. Tried and
  turned down: a gate swung open (an animal could walk round it -- a door, not a gap), wood touching a post
  (read as a small gate), rails laid tidy along the line, splinters and stubs (debris). The empty cells'
  yellow bar sprites are hidden (`empty_space.gd`).
- **The pen** (`pen_floor.gdshader` on each compound tile's `PipeFloor2`, its own material, told its
  `cell` in `pipe.gd` `set_rot`): trampled earth, hoof-pocked, with SPARSE straw stalks in drifts (dense, it
  was noise). It ignores the per-level color the pen used to be painted in.
- **The cells as faint lines** (2026-11-13, `pasture.gdshader`, told the board's `origin` and `tile` by
  `pipe.gd`): a thin soft seam, a little darker, between every two cells, anti-aliased to about a screen
  pixel and BROKEN so it blends -- each line wobbles, varies in strength and fades out in gaps; the pen's bedding has the same seams, fainter (`pen_floor.gdshader`). The animals move cell to
  cell, and with no grid to be seen their square turns looked arbitrary. Tried first: a mown checkerboard
  (and long before, mowing stripes, which read as bands across the board).
- **Farm props** (`farm_prop.gd`, `WolvesFarmProp`): a "brick" is a HAY BALE, a BOULDER or a WATER TROUGH,
  seen from above on a soft shadow. Still a brick to the game.
- **The woods** (`forest.gdshader`, `level._plant_forest` from `_fit_ground_to_board`): the lawn runs on
  past the fence and DARKENS into the trees' shade, the canopy closing in further out as soft irregular
  masses -- no single trees. RENDERED ONCE per board into a texture at a third of the resolution (it is
  many noise lookups a pixel; run every frame over the whole screen it cost a phone its frame rate), drawn
  with premultiplied alpha. Round tree crowns were tried first: on a phone, a children's playground.

## To the screen's foot (2026-11-11)

The lawn reaches as far as the zoomed-out game camera sees: `_fit_ground_to_board` passes
`game_cam` to `GrassField.fit`, and `zoom_camera` refits after setting it. On a phone the camera
shows more than one screen of world and the lawn stopped short of the foot (2026-11-11).
